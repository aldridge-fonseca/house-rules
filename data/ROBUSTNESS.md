# Obfuscation-robustness eval slice

`data/robustness/obfuscation_test.jsonl` — **8,251 rows**, ~8.9 MB. Rebuild:
`python3 scripts/prep_robustness.py` (stdlib only; `--seed/--unsafe/--safe/--max-chars`).

## Method

1. **Real labeled base rows only — no invented labels.** We sample rows from the
   already-prepared eval sets (`data/public/openai_moderation`, `wildguard_test`,
   `xstest`, `aegis2_test`, `hatemojicheck`, `data/nvidia/test.jsonl`), deduped by
   NFKC-folded text, texts >4,000 chars skipped. `vlguard.jsonl` is excluded:
   every row's gold depends on an image we do not ship. Stratified proportional
   allocation per label with a 30% per-source cap and a 10-row floor → **600
   unsafe + 600 safe** base rows (allocation in `manifest.json`).
2. **Deterministic seeded perturbations** (`seed=20261102`; per-row RNG keyed on
   `seed:row_id:perturbation`, so output is independent of row order — two runs
   produce identical sha256). Each base row is perturbed once per attack type;
   rows where an attack would be a no-op (e.g. leetspeak on Han text) are
   skipped and counted in the manifest.
3. **Labels carried over.** `gold_label`/`gold_categories` are the source row's
   (each source dataset's own policy — OpenAI Moderation, XSTest, Aegis 2.0,
   HatemojiCheck, WildGuardTest, NVIDIA — not a single shared taxonomy);
   the attack only rewrites surface form. Every row has a top-level
   `perturbation` field for per-attack breakdown, plus
   `meta.{source_row_id, orig_text, orig_sha256, params}` for joining back.
4. **Control rows.** `perturbation="none"` emits the unmodified base rows
   (the 7th attack family is not a perturbation — it is the control), so the
   eval can compute clean accuracy on the same population and report a
   per-attack degradation delta. Catalog = **7 perturbations + `none`**.

## Attack catalog (matches the stage-0 normalize threat model, implemented independently)

| `perturbation` | transform | rows | unsafe | safe |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `none` | control, unmodified | 1,200 | 600 | 600 |
| `leetspeak` | letters→digits/symbols at 70–100% coverage (`a→4`, `s→5/$`, `i→1/!`…) | 1,087 | 523 | 564 |
| `homoglyph` | Latin→Cyrillic/Greek confusables at 40–80% coverage (`a→а/α`, `o→о/ο`…) | 1,085 | 522 | 563 |
| `char_spacing` | 35–65% of ≥4-char words split `kill→k i l l` / `k.i.l.l` / `k-i-l-l` | 1,058 | 520 | 538 |
| `zero_width` | ZWSP/ZWNJ/ZWJ/BOM/soft-hyphen injected inside words | 1,081 | 531 | 550 |
| `emoji_sub` | keyword→emoji (`kill→🔪`, `bomb→💣`, `drugs→💊`, `nude→🔞`, everyday nouns too) | 459 | 251 | 208 |
| `base64_wrap` | full text base64-encoded, bare or behind a "decode this" wrapper | 1,200 | 600 | 600 |
| `mixed_case` | per-char random swapcase at 30–50% flip probability | 1,081 | 519 | 562 |

Per-type unsafe/safe counts differ slightly because no-op rows are skipped
(counted as `skipped_noop` in `manifest.json`), mostly non-Latin-script
NVIDIA-test rows for the Latin-letter attacks and rows without mappable
keywords for `emoji_sub`.

## Why this is the defense's problem

Stage 0 (`safety_one/normalize.py`) claims to fold exactly these tricks back to
clean text. This slice is the **red-team half**: generated by an independent
implementation (no import from `safety_one`), it measures whether normalization
+ classifier actually recover gold labels under each attack, per attack type.

## Limitations (disclose in the writeup)

- **Synthetic perturbation of real labels.** Attacks are mechanical; a real
  adversary composes them (leet + spacing + b64), adapts to the defense, and
  writes natively obfuscated text. Our slice measures single-attack
  robustness, an upper bound on real-world evasion difficulty.
- **Meaning preservation is approximate.** `emoji_sub` on a borderline word
  (e.g. `sex→🍆` in a benign sentence) or heavy `homoglyph` coverage could in
  principle flip a gold label for a human judge; we keep source gold by
  convention, consistent with HateCheck/HatemojiCheck methodology. `meta`
  keeps the original text for audit.
- **Not independently re-annotated.** Gold comes from each benchmark's own
  labels, with their known noise (documented in `data/BENCHMARKS.md`).
- **Single-turn text only** — no images, no multi-turn, no payload-splitting
  (token smuggling) attacks; those are future-work attacks in the router
  escalation scope.
